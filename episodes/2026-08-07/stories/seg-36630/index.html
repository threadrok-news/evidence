Full Narration

Spain has reinstated border controls targeting Italy, and here's why that's unusual: Spain and Italy don't share a land border. The controls are a diplomatic response to a dispute over migrants, roughly 78,000 of whom entered the Spanish exclave of Ceuta from Morocco. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez is directing the measures at Italian Premier Giorgia Meloni's government, with the row intensifying over responsibility for the Ceuta influx. The BBC has reported the border friction now extends to formal controls on movement between the two countries. Watch for whether other EU member states weigh in. The controls signal a breakdown in bilateral cooperation that goes beyond migration policy alone.
