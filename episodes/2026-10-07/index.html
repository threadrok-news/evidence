Canada's federal government announced Wednesday it will legislate to prevent people whose sole health condition is mental illness from accessing assisted dying, suspending a planned March 2027 expansion indefinitely.

Full Narration

Canada is indefinitely suspending plans to expand assisted dying to people whose sole health condition is mental illness. The expansion had been scheduled for March 2027. The federal government moved Wednesday to advance legislation that would block that pathway entirely. Here's the context: under current rules, Canadians with a mental illness as their only condition can already access euthanasia. The new legislation, according to BBC News, would close that access, not restrict it from expanding, but reverse what is already on the books. The question now is how that legislation moves through Parliament and whether the indefinite pause holds or becomes permanent. Watch for the bill's timeline in coming weeks as the March 2027 deadline recedes.
